Why Explore Himachal

Dev Bhoomi — the land of the gods.

Himachal Pradesh sits in the Western Himalayas, earning its nickname 'Dev Bhoomi' from the sheer number of historical temples scattered across the state. Shimla, Manali, Dharamshala, Dalhousie, Chamba, Khajjiar, Kullu and Kasauli are its best-known hill stations.

It's also home to major Hindu pilgrimage sites — Naina Devi, Bajreshwari Mata, Jwala Ji, Chintpurni, Chamunda Devi, Baijnath, Bhimakali and Jakhoo temples among them.

Must-Visit Spots

Things to do in Himachal.

From Shimla's colonial charm to Manali's adventure sports — the essentials of every LeisureFlies Himachal package.

Shimla Local Sightseeing

Christ Church, The Mall Road, Shimla State Museum, Sankat Mochan Temple and Summer Hill.

Kalka-Shimla Toy Train

A UNESCO World Heritage narrow-gauge railway — a 5.5-hour ride through deodar and pine forests.

Kufri

16km from Shimla — ski slopes, a mini zoo, and views over the Potato Research Station.

Paragliding in Manali

At 2050m above sea level, Manali is a favorite for both international and domestic paragliders.

Solang Valley

Parachuting, paragliding, horse riding, and zorbing — one of Himachal's most popular adventure hubs.

Rohtang Pass

Open June-October, the gateway to Lahaul & Spiti — sledging, snow scooters, and the Rahala Waterfall nearby.

Good to Know

Best time to visit?

March to June, and September to December.

Safety near rivers

Water currents in Himachal's rivers are strong — avoid standing near cliffs or riverbanks for photos.

Free advice

Carry cash — ATMs get scarce in remote areas.

Himachal in Numbers

Village of Taboos

Malana village has a strict rule: no outsider may touch a resident's belongings or the village walls.

Seven hills

Shimla sits atop seven hills, including Jakhu Hill and Summer Hill.

A historic church

Christ Church (built 1857) is India's second-oldest church, after St. John's in Meerut.
